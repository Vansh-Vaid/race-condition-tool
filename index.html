How it decides

    

      
Candidate
Two operations in different threads touch the same variable, and at least one writes. These are Bernstein’s conditions. Two reads never conflict.

      
Locked
Both threads hold a common mutex at that moment (the Eraser lockset rule). A semaphore that starts at 1 counts too, if every thread waits before it signals.

      
Ordered
A WAIT that cannot pass without a SIGNAL puts everything before the SIGNAL ahead of everything after the WAIT. Start the semaphore at 0 for this.

      
Can collide
Neither rule applies, and a search of every schedule found a moment when both could run. “Show it happen” takes you there.

      
Never meet
The rules cannot prove it safe, but no schedule brings the two together.

      
Values
Every variable starts at 0. A WRITE after a READ stores the copy plus 1. A WRITE on its own stores the thread’s number.

    

    
On the map, time runs right. A train carries the copy it read. A line that trembles is unprotected, and a sheathed track is holding a lock. 

    
Why a railway? Dijkstra’s semaphore is named after the railway signal. A mutex is the token a driver must hold to enter a single line, and a deadlock is two trains facing each other, each waiting for the other’s section.
